# Literacy Sources — AI Literacy Presentation

Reference material for the "Foundational AI Literacy for Research Administrators"
concurrent session at NCURA Annual 2026.

Structured in two parts:

1. **Traditional literacy definitions** — used to *ground* the argument. AI
   literacy is presented as the same continuum applied to a new medium.
2. **AI / digital literacy definitions** — used as the natural evolution of #1.

---

## Part 1 — Traditional Literacy Definitions (the foundation)

Anchor of the framing. Literacy has never been "just decoding text" — global
bodies have defined it in terms of participation, agency, and empowerment for
decades.

### UNESCO (the anchor)

> "Literacy is now understood as a means of identification, understanding,
> interpretation, creation, and communication in an increasingly digital,
> text-mediated, information-rich and fast-changing world. Literacy is a
> continuum of learning and proficiency in reading, writing and using numbers
> throughout life..."

- **Source:** UNESCO, *What you need to know about literacy* (updated 2026).
  <https://www.unesco.org/en/literacy/need-know>
- **Angle:** Continuum framing; explicitly names digital context.

### UNESCO (functional literacy — the operative UN definition)

> "A person is functionally literate who can engage in all those activities in
> which literacy is required for effective functioning of his group and
> community and also for enabling him to continue to use reading, writing and
> calculation for his own and the community's development."

- **Source:** UNESCO General Conference, *Revised Recommendation concerning
  the International Standardization of Educational Statistics* (1978);
  reaffirmed by UIS.
- **Angle:** Community empowerment and continuous development.

### OECD / PIAAC (Survey of Adult Skills)

> "The ability to understand, evaluate, use and engage with written texts to
> participate in society, to achieve one's goals, and to develop one's
> knowledge and potential."

- **Source:** OECD, *PIAAC Literacy Framework* (2009/2012, retained 2023 cycle).
  <https://nces.ed.gov/surveys/piaac/measure.asp>
- **Angle:** Active participation and agency. OECD explicitly upgraded
  "functioning in society" → "participate in society" to signal a more active
  role.

### U.S. NAAL / NCES

> "Using printed and written information to function in society, to achieve
> one's goals, and to develop one's knowledge and potential."

- **Source:** U.S. Dept. of Education, National Center for Education Statistics,
  *National Assessment of Adult Literacy* (2003). <https://nces.ed.gov/naal/>
- **Angle:** Functional / practical. U.S. federal operating definition.

### International Literacy Association (ILA)

> "The ability to identify, understand, interpret, create, compute, and
> communicate using visual, audible, and digital materials across disciplines
> and in any context."

- **Source:** ILA, *Literacy Glossary* (2018–present).
  <https://www.literacyworldwide.org/get-resources/literacy-glossary>
- **Angle:** Multi-modal and cross-contextual. Natural bridge to "literacy for
  a new medium."

### National Literacy Trust (UK)

> "The ability to read, write, speak and listen in a way that lets us
> communicate effectively and make sense of the world."

- **Source:** National Literacy Trust, *What is Literacy?*
  <https://literacytrust.org.uk/information/what-is-literacy/>
- **Angle:** Sense-making — literacy as the tool for interpreting the world.

### ProLiteracy

> "The ability to read, write, compute, and use technology at a level that
> enables an individual to reach his or her full potential as a parent,
> employee, and community member."

- **Source:** ProLiteracy, *About / Defining Literacy.*
  <https://www.proliteracy.org/about/>
- **Angle:** Full-potential / role-based empowerment.

### Paulo Freire (intellectual anchor)

> "Reading the world always precedes reading the word, and reading the word
> implies continually reading the world."

- **Source:** Freire & Macedo, *Literacy: Reading the Word and the World*
  (Bergin & Garvey, 1987); idea originates in *Pedagogy of the Oppressed*
  (1970).
- **Angle:** Critical / liberatory. Literacy is inseparable from critically
  interpreting one's reality — an act of agency, not consumption.

### Suggested framing arc

Freire (critical/liberatory) → UNESCO 1978 (community functioning) → NAAL
(personal potential) → PIAAC (active participation) → ILA (any medium, any
context) → **therefore** AI literacy is the same continuum applied to a new
medium.

## Part 2 — AI / Digital Literacy Definitions (the next chapter)

Reference only. Use sparingly. UNESCO's digital-literacy definition already
does the front-and-center work on the page.

### UNESCO (digital literacy — currently the front-and-center quote on the page)

> "The ability to access, manage, understand, integrate, communicate,
> evaluate and create information safely and appropriately through digital
> technologies for employment, decent jobs and entrepreneurship... [including
> the ability to] build resilience in the face of disinformation, hate speech
> and violent extremism."

- **Source:** UNESCO, *What you need to know about literacy* (updated 2026).

### OECD × European Commission — *Empowering Learners for the Age of AI*

> "AI literacy, as a set of knowledge, skills and attitudes, equips learners
> to understand how AI systems work, critically evaluate their outputs and
> use them ethically and creatively."

- **Source:** OECD & European Commission, *Empowering Learners for the Age
  of AI* (2025). <https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/empowering-learners-for-the-age-of-ai_65cd27d4-en.html>
- **Angle:** Ethical + creative use + mechanistic understanding.

### Long & Magerko (ACM CHI 2020)

> "A set of competencies that enables individuals to critically evaluate AI
> technologies; communicate and collaborate effectively with AI; and use AI
> as a tool online, at home, and in the workplace."

- **Source:** Long & Magerko, "What is AI Literacy? Competencies and Design
  Considerations," ACM CHI 2020.
  <https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3313831.3376727>
- **Angle:** Scholarly canon. Introduces *collaboration with* AI — reframes
  AI as a collaborator, not just a tool to evaluate.

### World Economic Forum — Future of Jobs 2025

> "AI and big data" tops the list of fastest-growing skills through 2030,
> alongside "technological literacy," "analytical thinking," and "resilience,
> flexibility and agility."

- **Source:** WEF, *Future of Jobs Report 2025* (Jan 2025).
  <https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025/in-full/3-skills-outlook/>
- **Angle:** Labor-market urgency, quantified.

### UNICEF — Policy Guidance on AI for Children

- Calls for "equipping children with digital literacy to use AI safely and
  critically" alongside "understanding of AI's capabilities and limitations."
- **Source:** UNICEF, *Policy Guidance on AI for Children 2.0* (2021; v3.0
  update 2025). <https://www.unicef.org/innocenti/reports/policy-guidance-ai-children>
- **Angle:** Rights-based; adds *capabilities and limitations* framing.

### IFLA — *Developing a Library Strategic Response to AI*

- AI literacy "encompassing data and algorithmic literacies... a natural role
  for libraries extending their promotion of information literacy and digital
  skills."
- **Source:** IFLA (2024). <https://www.ifla.org/g/ai/developing-a-library-strategic-response-to-artificial-intelligence/>
- **Angle:** Stacks AI literacy with data + algorithmic literacies.

### U.S. Dept. of Education — *AI and the Future of Teaching and Learning*

> Frames AI as "a rapidly advancing class of foundational capabilities which
> are increasingly embedded in all types of educational technology systems,"
> calling for AI literacy integrated across teaching practice.

- **Source:** Office of Educational Technology (May 2023).
  <https://www.ed.gov/sites/ed/files/documents/ai-report/ai-report.pdf>
- **Angle:** U.S. federal policy anchor.

### ITU — *Digital Skills Assessment Guidebook*

> Digital skills exist "along a continuum ranging from basic to intermediate
> to advanced."

- **Source:** ITU (2020). <https://academy.itu.int/sites/default/files/media2/file/20-00227_1f_Digital_Skills_assessment_Guidebook_%2028%20May%202020.pdf>
- **Angle:** Continuum/maturity framing.

### World Bank — *Digital Skills Development* (WP 14)

- Digital literacy is "a foundational domain that equips individuals to
  utilize digital technologies in daily life and work settings," including
  "information and data literacy."
- **Source:** World Bank Working Paper No. 14 (2025).
- **Angle:** Development-economics framing.

### EU DigComp (European Commission / JRC)

> "The confident, critical and responsible use of, and engagement with,
> digital technologies for learning, at work, and for participation in
> society... a combination of knowledge, skills and attitudes."

- **Source:** European Commission, *Council Recommendation on Key
  Competences for Lifelong Learning* (2018); DigComp 3.0 (2025).
- **Angle:** EU's canonical K-S-A competency triad.

### IEEE — P7015 (in development)

- Standard for Data and AI Literacy, Skills, and Readiness.
- **Angle:** Engineering-standards community formalizing literacy as
  measurable readiness. Worth naming to show the pattern isn't just policy.
