# Deck Outline: Foundational AI Literacy for Research Administrators

Proposed shape for the NCURA Annual 2026 session. Arc: literacy → digital literacy → AI literacy → course modules.

## Part 1: Why Literacy

**Argument:** literacy has never meant decoding text. It has always meant participation, agency, and sense-making. The word has been continuously redefined by UNESCO, OECD, and others as the world changes.

- **Slide 1.1** Section header. "Literacy is agency."
- **Slide 1.2** Freire quote: "Reading the world always precedes reading the word." Pair with PIAAC or NAAL: literacy as participation in society, achieving one's goals, developing one's potential. Message: the verbs are the same across every era.

## Part 2: Why Digital Literacy

**Argument:** the internet did not replace literacy. It extended the same verbs to a new medium. UNESCO's digital literacy definition uses the exact same words as its literacy definition, applied to digital technologies.

- **Slide 2.1** Section header. "Same verbs. New medium."
- **Slide 2.2** UNESCO digital literacy quote (already on current deck, keep it). Highlight the seven verbs: access, manage, understand, integrate, communicate, evaluate, create. Add the disinformation-resilience clause.

## Part 3: Why AI Literacy

**Argument:** AI is the next new medium. The seven verbs still apply. But AI has a property no prior medium had: the output is inferred, not specified in advance. That changes what "literacy" has to include.

- **Slide 3.1** Section header. "The next chapter."
- **Slide 3.2** AI literacy definitions from OECD/EC (2025) and Long & Magerko (2020). Same seven verbs. Add: *evaluate outputs critically* and *collaborate with*.
- **Slide 3.3** What's actually new about AI vs prior media. Behavior is inferred from data, not specified. You can approve the tool and the guardrails, never the output. Assurance moves to processes around the tool.

## Part 5: The Course

**Argument:** the AI4RA course operationalizes the seven verbs into five modules. Each module builds one competency.

- **Slide 5.1** Section header. "Five modules, one arc." (Optional preview: 5 modules, 2-3 hours, asynchronous, free via NCURA, certificate on completion.)
- **Slide 5.2 — Module 1: Introduction to AI in RA.** Behavior inferred from data. Crowd of amateurs metaphor. AI use continuum: process-critical, human-in-the-loop, output-driven. AI task vs. data science task.
- **Slide 5.3 — Module 2: Ethical and Responsible AI Use.** Four guiding questions (vulnerability, harm, benefit, reversibility). Two methodologies (analogy, reflective equilibrium). Three collective-action structures. Bias, privacy (FERPA/HIPAA/GDPR), disclosure, sponsor guidance (NSF/NIH/OSTP).
- **Slide 5.4 — Module 3: Prompt Engineering.** Seven components of a good prompt (TaMPER framework). Context window as page limit. Three disciplines: prompt engineering, context engineering, conversation management. Promptulus.
- **Slide 5.5 — Module 4: Compliance, Risk, Governance.** Compliance Pyramid. Multi-level governance (institution, sponsor, state, federal). 5W1H for policy. Data classification drives tool choice. Export controls, NSPM-33.
- **Slide 5.6 — Module 5: AI Use Cases.** Concrete RA examples: budget justifications, plain-language checks, translating technical narrative, award terms review (poisoned-PDF), checklist automation, progress reports, contract review. This is the "here's what it looks like in your job" slide.
